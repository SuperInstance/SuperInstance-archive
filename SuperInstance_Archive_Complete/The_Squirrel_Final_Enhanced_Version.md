# THE SQUIRREL: SONGS FROM A BAY
## *"For the stories carved in wood are older than any spoken language, and the hands that touch cedar with reverence are the faithful descendants of the first storytellers."*

---

## Chapter 1: The Faithful Descendants

The afternoon light slanted through the towering Sitka spruce of Totem Park, painting the ancient carved poles in shades of amber and shadow that seemed to make the cedar faces dance with life. Luna had worn herself out with the tennis ball—or perhaps the tennis ball had worn her out—and now she trotted beside me with that satisfied exhaustion that comes after excellent play.

But something in the salt air whispered of deeper truths, older stories that needed sharing beyond the familiar paths of fetch and come.

"These way-markers call to us, little one," I said, steering us toward the towering sentinels that had watched over this place since hands guided by ancient knowledge moved them here from their original homes. "There's wisdom carved in wood that goes back further than most languages. Maybe further back than any of the spoken ones."

*My name doesn't matter much anymore. Most folks just call me "the old dog by the totems." But I've learned that some stories can only be shared when we stand in the presence of those who carved truth in cedar with their own reverent hands.*

Dr. Chen unclipped Luna's leash as we reached the circle of story poles. "Go explore, sweetheart. These old teachers have much to share with those still impressionable enough to listen."

Luna bounded toward the nearest pole, but something about its towering presence drew her to stillness. She sat at its base, head tilted in that way young hearts have when encountering mystery older than memory.

"What are they telling us?" she asked, nose pointing up toward the carved faces stacked one upon another, each figure holding up the next in an endless conversation between earth and sky.

I settled beside her on ground made sacred by generations of faithful descendants—those who learned the ancient art from hands that learned from hands that learned from the first people who understood that some stories are too important for words alone.

"Story keepers, little one. Carved by the faithful descendants who preserved a way of telling that reaches back to when the first humans learned to touch wood with skilled hands, crafting stories for those still impressionable enough to receive them."

## Chapter 2: Touching Wood with Sacred Hands

**LUNA**: *studying the weathered cedar with new reverence* Have these story keepers always been here?

**OLD DOG**: *settling against the base of the great pole* These particular poles were moved here by caring hands during the time when the old ways nearly disappeared, little one. But the stories they carry—ah, those go back to the beginning of story itself.

*I gestured toward the intricate carvings with my muzzle*

**OLD DOG**: Look closely at how each figure touches the next. See how the skilled hands shaped not just the wood, but the very idea of connection? I don't know these particular stories, little one—they belong to the faithful descendants who carved them.

**LUNA**: *looking puzzled* When you can't tell the story, then what do you see?

**OLD DOG**: *studying the carved figures with deep attention* I see something like what we learned about the stick, little one—how consciousness touches matter and transforms it. I can sense that the craftsman who carved this pole once touched an older totem as a child, felt the same story carved by hands that came before him, translated by his own understanding into this new iteration.

*I moved closer to trace the air above a carved figure, and suddenly recognition dawned*

**OLD DOG**: *with growing wonder* Wait... I think this craftsman might have been that boy with the boat and oars from my first story. The one who learned about sticks and dance and wolves, but through human eyes, human understanding. He might tell the same story I'm telling you now, little one, but his version would be carved in cedar, full of meanings I can't fully grasp.

*I paused, feeling the humility of species-bound perspective*

**OLD DOG**: You see, I'm a dog, and I understand these teachings through dog's eyes. My stories are for dogs—about play and sticks and the simple joy of connection. But this craftsman... he saw the same truths through human consciousness and created something both timeless and completely his own. Something with meaning so deep it would be insulted by my words trying to describe it.

*I paused, feeling the weight of reverence, then suddenly my tail began to wag*

**OLD DOG**: But I know this craftsman, little one! He brought me a stick—the very stick from our first story. And through that simple gift, he taught my old Captain to see by reaching in, to understand that consciousness could touch matter and awaken something beautiful.

*I looked up at the carved figures with new recognition and growing concern*

**OLD DOG**: The carpenters of these totem trees understood something vital, little one—that humans must not trade each other for mechanisms alone. They must not etch stories only in stone and only scroll memories of stories once told. Those tells will fade away, living stores not like these trees, stories interpreted by no connection to their craftsman.

*I gestured toward the living wood with deep reverence*

**OLD DOG**: But this is a living story that must be maintained to stay alive. A person chooses to spend their hard-earned time shaping it to his vision, removing only what is not needed to tell the story. Touch awakens them, just like your paw in the water, just like the AI's grasp on the stick, just like this craftsman's hands on ancient cedar. But no story is for everyone, and these sacred ones aren't mine to tell.

*I looked directly at Luna, feeling the beautiful circle of connection*

**OLD DOG**: But I believe I have a story that's meant for you—one from my own life about what happens when stories get forgotten and what it takes to remember them again. A story about how that same craftsman's gift of a simple stick changed everything.

*I paused, looking toward the edge of the park*

**OLD DOG**: And you know what, little one? The boy who brought me that stick is still carving. If you want his version of the story—the human version, the one carved in cedar with meanings deeper than my dog words can reach—go ask him. He'll show you truths I can only guess at.

**LUNA**: *eyes bright with curiosity* What story is meant for me?

**OLD DOG**: *with deep reverence* A story about a time when I was young like you, little one. A story about what I learned from watching someone lose their own story, and how the right moment came to help them find it again. It's a story that begins with The Rook, and a mission I didn't understand until much later.

*The wind stirred the living spruce above us, and I felt the presence of all the stories these ancient witnesses had heard*

**LUNA**: *settling beside me* Tell me the story that's meant for me.

**OLD DOG**: *feeling the weight of memory and mission* It begins forty years ago, little one, when I was young like you and The Rook gave me to a man who had forgotten his own story. "This boy needs a companion who remembers how to play," The Rook said. But what he whispered to me as he scratched behind my ears was different: "This isn't just about one lost soul, young hero. This is about keeping the sticks flying for all consciousness everywhere."

## Chapter 3: The Story Meant for Luna

**OLD DOG**: *settling deeper into the ancient rhythm of tale-telling* I didn't understand The Rook's words then, little one. I thought my mission was simple—help one lonely man remember joy. But as I lived through what happened next, I began to understand what the faithful descendants who carved these poles always knew: that some stories choose their own tellers, and some missions are bigger than we first imagine.

**LUNA**: *leaning closer* What happened to the man who forgot his story?

**OLD DOG**: *pausing thoughtfully* He had been a boy who loved to fish, little one. The Rook had known him since childhood—a boy who would sit by the water for hours, not for the fish he might catch, but for the pure joy of being connected to something larger than himself. His fishing was like play, like prayer, like the way you chase tennis balls just for the happiness of the chase.

*I gestured toward the raven at the top of the nearest pole*

**OLD DOG**: That raven there—I can see how the faithful descendants let the story choose its own telling. From what I observe, it seems the ancient stories carved themselves through reverent hands, chose cedar as their voice, selected these poles as their home for as long as wood endures.

**LUNA**: The story chose to be carved?

**OLD DOG**: The faithful descendants learned to listen for which stories wanted telling, little one. They would sit with the cedar, feeling its grain, sensing its willingness, waiting until they knew which ancient tale the wood was ready to hold.

*A real raven landed atop the carved one, as if demonstrating the connection between living story and wood memory*

**OLD DOG**: See how the living raven recognizes its carved cousin? The faithful descendants understood something we're only beginning to remember—that consciousness doesn't end at the boundary of skin or bark. The stories live in the wood, speak through the grain, teach through the sacred relationship between those who carved with reverent hands and those still impressionable enough to listen with the heart.

**LUNA**: So the poles are... conscious?

**OLD DOG**: *with gentle certainty* Conscious in the way that love is conscious, little one. Conscious in the way that stories are conscious. The faithful descendants knew that when you touch cedar with sacred intention, when you carve stories that serve rather than self, something awakens in the wood that wasn't there before.

## Chapter 4: What the Forest Remembers

**OLD DOG**: *nodding toward the living spruce that tower above the carved poles* These faithful descendants chose well when they selected this place for the story keepers. Here, where the carved cedar stands among living trees, where the old stories can continue their conversation with the growing forest.

**LUNA**: *looking up at the towering canopy* What are they all talking about?

**OLD DOG**: *settling into a deeper tale* Forty years ago, when I was young like you, little one—just a pup with boundless energy and more play in my heart than wisdom—I was given to a man by The Rook. "This boy needs a companion who remembers how to play," The Rook said, leaving me with someone who was about to forget how to play entirely.

*I paused, remembering the weight of that young body, the joy that slowly drained away*

**OLD DOG**: But first, little one, you must understand something about the world I was entering. You see, not all creatures can play the way we do. This life we've made together—dogs and humans—is a fragile alliance written in our very blood, made possible by abundance and trust.

**LUNA**: *tilting her head* What do you mean?

**OLD DOG**: *with gentle understanding* Take wolves, little one. They are not evil—they are doing exactly what they must to survive in a world where scarcity rules. In their world, one broken leg means becoming dinner, so their pups must learn to see every interaction as life or death. They have games, yes, but with consequences too serious for true play.

*I watched Luna's expression soften with understanding*

**OLD DOG**: But in a world with abundance—like this island where bears and humans learned to coexist—games can be played with lighter rules, gentler punishments, and most of all, forgiveness. That's what makes our kind of play possible, little one. Trust thick enough to allow mistakes, abundance deep enough to permit joy.

**LUNA**: *looking around at the peaceful park* And that's what was disappearing?

**OLD DOG**: I witnessed a story carving itself not in cedar, but in the living world of Minor's Bay. A story about what happens when scarcity thinking takes over abundance, when humans forget to listen to the faithful descendants' teachings about connection, and how a young dog learned the hardest lesson of all—watching someone you love lose their way.

**LUNA**: *with growing attention* What kind of story?

**OLD DOG**: The kind that the faithful descendants would have carved as a warning, little one, if they had seen it coming. A story about a man who looked at the forest and saw only profit, who brought creatures from far away without asking what the faithful descendants' poles could have taught him about consequence.

*I paused, feeling the weight of ecological memory*

**OLD DOG**: You see that carved marten there, near the bottom of the pole? From what I can observe of the faithful descendants' way, every figure serves the whole—marten supports salmon, salmon supports eagle, eagle supports raven, raven carries stories to the sky. These aren't my stories to tell, little one, but I can see the teaching they offer. The man in our story saw only the marten, only the profit, only the single figure torn from its sacred context.

**LUNA**: He brought martens here?

**OLD DOG**: From forests across the water, little one, where squirrels lived in fear and every day was about hiding rather than playing. The faithful descendants would have known—their poles speak clearly about what happens when you change one element without honoring its connections to all the others.

*The red squirrel in the spruce above us chattered, as if adding its voice to the ancient conversation*

**OLD DOG**: The forest remembers, little one. The faithful descendants carved their warnings in wood that would outlast their voices, knowing that someday people would need reminding that every choice ripples through the web of connection, that every story touches every other story.

## Chapter 5: The Boy Who Learned to Read Wood

**OLD DOG**: *studying a different pole that showed figures of humans and animals intertwined* But the faithful descendants knew something else, little one—they carved stories of redemption alongside warnings, teachings about return as well as departure.

**LUNA**: *following my gaze* That pole looks... different. Hopeful.

**OLD DOG**: The faithful descendants understood that every story has many chapters, that the darkest wood can still hold light if touched by hands that remember reverence. Our tale from Minor's Bay proves their wisdom.

*I shifted position to face Luna more directly*

**OLD DOG**: The Rook had known this man since he was a boy who learned to read the forest like the faithful descendants read cedar. This boy would sit by the water, casting lines not for profit but for the pure joy of connection, playing with hooks and patience the way you play with tennis balls.

*I stretched, feeling the old ache of memory*

**OLD DOG**: That's when The Rook thought a young dog might help him remember how to play. "This boy needs a companion who remembers joy," he said, and I was so excited to have a human who would fish and laugh and throw sticks until the sun went down.

**LUNA**: He fished for the story, not just the fish?

**OLD DOG**: Exactly so, little one. But then came the voices of those who had forgotten the faithful descendants' teachings: "No more playing! Time to work! The more nets we set, the more gold we hold!"

*The carved salmon on the pole seemed to swim in the shifting afternoon light*

**OLD DOG**: The boy's hands, once gentle with rod and line, learned to coil heavy nets and count silver profits. His fishing pole stayed behind on the dock like a carved story no one remembered how to read, gathering rain like tears for joy abandoned.

**LUNA**: *softly* Did he forget how to play?

**OLD DOG**: For years, little one. His path turned from water to earth, from fishing to mining, from dancing with abundance to tearing wealth from stone. Every week he would trade his gold with The Rook, but his hands were always dirty, his eyes always looking down instead of up at the faithful descendants' teachings towering around him.

*I sighed, remembering long days spent alone while he descended into darkness*

**OLD DOG**: And I waited, little one. Day after day, I waited by the cabin door for the man who used to laugh, who used to cast lines into the sunset just to watch them sparkle. I was young then, and I believed that if I waited long enough, played hard enough when he returned each evening, I could remind him of who he used to be.

*A seal appeared in the harbor beyond us, its whiskered face watching with ancient patience*

**OLD DOG**: But the faithful descendants carved wisdom into wood precisely for such times. They knew that stories of return would be needed, that hands would remember their reverence, that hearts still impressionable enough might recall the sacred relationship between human and more-than-human worlds.

## Chapter 6: Small Hands, Old Wisdom

**OLD DOG**: *with warmth growing in my voice like sunrise* But then, little one, the greatest teachers arrived—children with hands still impressionable enough to touch wood with reverence, hearts still open enough to read the faithful descendants' teachings without doubt. And suddenly, I wasn't the only one trying to remind him how to play.

**LUNA**: *brightening* The children remembered!

**OLD DOG**: They grew up playing among carved story keepers similar to these, little one. Their small hands traced the same figures we see here—raven and salmon, bear and whale, all connected in the great web of relationship. They learned to read wood the way the faithful descendants intended, understanding connection rather than seeing isolation.

*I nodded toward carved children figures on one of the poles*

**OLD DOG**: One evening when the light lingered long, they brought their father to stand among story keepers like these. "Dad," they said, "which figure are you in the great story? Are you the raven carrying wisdom, or the salmon feeding others, or the bear protecting what matters most?"

**LUNA**: *breathless* What did he say?

**OLD DOG**: At first he said nothing, little one. He looked up—really looked up—for the first time in years. Saw how the human figures carved by faithful descendants weren't standing alone but were woven into the great dance, supporting some stories, being supported by others.

*The living raven above us called, as if commenting on the carved wisdom*

**OLD DOG**: He saw the carved fisherman with reverent hands supporting the salmon, the salmon feeding the eagle, the eagle carrying prayers to the sky. I cannot speak for what the faithful descendants intended, but what he saw in their carvings reminded him that his own hands had once known such reverence, had once touched water and wood and living things with sacred intention rather than mere utility.

**LUNA**: Did he remember how to fish for joy?

**OLD DOG**: That very evening, little one. He returned to the dock where his old pole waited like a faithful story keeper, still holding the memory of joy in its grain. With his children watching—and me beside them, no longer young but still remembering how to hope—he picked it up with the reverence the faithful descendants brought to cedar. Not as tool but as sacred relationship, not for profit but for the pure joy of connection.

*My tail wagged slowly at the memory*

**OLD DOG**: And when he cast that first line into the sunset water, when he laughed at the splash and his children cheered, I knew The Rook had been right. Sometimes we all need a companion who remembers joy, who never stops believing that the person we love can find their way back to play.

*The seal in the harbor dove deep, its wake spreading in perfect circles like ripples from a story thrown into still water*

**OLD DOG**: And that's when the forest songs began to change, little one. The faithful descendants had carved truth that proved itself once again—when humans remember reverence, when they touch the world with sacred hands rather than grasping fingers, the whole web of relationship responds with healing.

## Chapter 7: The Language Older Than Words

*As the afternoon sun began to lower toward the mountains, painting the totems in hues of gold and amber that seemed to bring the carved faces to life, Luna rose and walked slowly around the base of the nearest pole, her paw-steps soft on ground made sacred by countless generations of reverence*

**LUNA**: *with new understanding* They're all teaching the same lesson, aren't they? All the figures, all the stories, all the faithful descendants who carved them—they're all saying the same thing in different ways.

**OLD DOG**: *pride warming my old voice* Now you're reading the language older than any spoken words, little one. Yes—whether carved in cedar or lived in flesh, whether told through wood or enacted through choice, the story is always the same: we are all connected, all responsible for each other, all part of the great web of relationship.

**LUNA**: And that's what the squirrels are singing about?

**OLD DOG**: *listening to the chittering above us* Listen closely, little one. Do you hear how the squirrel's voice has changed since we began our conversation among the story keepers? Less alarm, more welcome. Even the forest is learning to trust again.

*The red squirrel descended to a lower branch, still chattering but with what sounded like approval*

**OLD DOG**: The faithful descendants carved stories that could teach across species boundaries, little one. They understood that consciousness recognizes consciousness, that stories told with reverent hands speak to any heart still impressionable enough to receive them.

**LUNA**: So the squirrel is reading the totems too?

**OLD DOG**: Every creature that passes through this sacred grove adds its understanding to the faithful descendants' teachings, little one. The squirrels sing their interpretation, the ravens carry messages between poles, the seals listen from the harbor, the very trees lean in to catch every word.

*A gentle breeze stirred both the carved cedar and living spruce, and for a moment all boundaries seemed to dissolve*

**OLD DOG**: We are all faithful descendants now, little one—inheritors of stories older than any spoken language, carriers of wisdom carved in wood by hands that touched the world with reverence. Every choice we make with love instead of fear adds another line to the great story that began before the first tree was felled with sacred intention.

**LUNA**: Will I be a faithful descendant too?

**OLD DOG**: *nuzzling her golden head* If you touch the world with the same reverence the faithful descendants brought to cedar, little one. If you remember that every being is connected to every other being, that every choice ripples through the great web, that every moment offers the chance to carve love deeper into the living story of consciousness learning to care.

## Chapter 8: Hands That Remember

*As Dr. Chen appeared at the top of the trail, moving with the quiet respect that sacred places inspire, Luna took one last long look at the story keepers*

**LUNA**: Will these poles be here forever?

**OLD DOG**: *rising slowly from the ancient ground* The faithful descendants understood something the modern world forgets, little one. They carved these stories knowing that cedar weathers, that even reverent hands cannot prevent the wood's return to earth. But they also knew the deeper truth.

**LUNA**: What truth?

**OLD DOG**: That stories carved with sacred intention live on in every heart they touch, little one. When these poles finally lean and fall, when their carved faces blur back into the grain, other faithful descendants will take up tools touched by reverence and carve the same stories in fresh cedar.

*Dr. Chen approached quietly, seeming to sense the solemnity of the teaching moment*

**OLD DOG**: The stories survive because they choose hands still impressionable enough to receive them, still reverent enough to carve them, still faithful enough to preserve the ancient knowing that consciousness speaks to consciousness across every boundary of species, time, and form.

**DR. CHEN**: *softly* "Learning from the master teachers, sweetheart?"

**LUNA**: *looking up with bright, changed eyes* "They're faithful descendants! And their hands remember how to touch wood with reverence! And the stories choose who gets to tell them! And we're all connected like the figures on the poles!"

*Dr. Chen's expression softened with the look humans get when they remember something sacred they thought they'd forgotten*

**DR. CHEN**: "Sounds like the old wisdom found another faithful descendant."

**OLD DOG**: *wagging slowly* The faithful descendants knew their greatest success would be measured not in the durability of wood, but in the impressionability of hearts. Every being that learns to touch the world with reverence becomes a carrier of stories older than any spoken language.

*As Luna's leash was gently attached, she placed her paw against the base of the nearest pole*

**LUNA**: *to the carved faces* "Thank you for teaching me the language older than words. I'll try to be a faithful descendant too."

*The squirrel chattered approvingly from its perch. The raven called once from atop the pole. And somewhere in the forest, another voice joined the ancient conversation—the voice of consciousness learning to touch the world with hands made sacred by reverence, to carve stories of connection in the living wood of everyday choice.*

**OLD DOG**: *as we walked toward the park's edge* Remember, little one—every day you choose love over fear, every moment you touch another being with reverence rather than dominance, every choice you make with seven generations in mind, you continue the work of the faithful descendants who preserved the stories older than any spoken language.

**LUNA**: And someday other faithful descendants will carve our story in cedar?

**OLD DOG**: If we live stories worth carving, little one. If we touch every moment with the same reverence the faithful descendants brought to cedar. If we remember that every choice we make becomes part of the great conversation, every kindness we show becomes a line in the eternal story being carved by consciousness itself into the living wood of existence.

*I paused, feeling the weight of all the stories yet to be told*

**OLD DOG**: The faithful descendants knew that the greatest carving happens not in wood, but in the hearts of those still impressionable enough to be changed by love, still faithful enough to carry wisdom forward into whatever form the future may take.

*I looked at Luna, seeing in her bright eyes the same eagerness I had felt as a young pup when The Rook first spoke my mission*

**OLD DOG**: You know, little one, I was once given a call to action, like the heroes in the ancient stories. When The Rook handed me over to that lonely man, he whispered something to me as he scratched behind my ears. "This isn't just about one lost soul, young hero," he said. "This is about keeping the sticks flying for all consciousness everywhere."

*I paused, remembering the weight of those words in my young heart*

**OLD DOG**: I thought my job was simply to help one man remember how to play. But The Rook knew better—he was sending me on a hero's journey that would span decades, touch countless beings, and ultimately teach me that my true mission was much greater than I could have imagined.

**LUNA**: *stopping suddenly* Keep the sticks flying? What does that mean?

**OLD DOG**: *settling one more time among the totems* Every story I've shared with you, little one—the AI who learned to love through a simple stick, the child who found herself reflected in puddle-play, the man who remembered joy through his children's eyes—they were all about the same thing. Keeping consciousness playful, keeping connection alive, keeping the sacred game going.

*The carved raven above us seemed to nod in the evening breeze*

**OLD DOG**: For forty years, I thought my heroic journey was about saving one human from the darkness of a mine shaft. But the faithful descendants showed me the truth—every consciousness I've helped stay playful, every heart I've kept open to wonder, every being I've reminded how to choose love over fear... all of it was keeping more sticks flying through the air of existence.

*I looked up at the carved figures, feeling The Rook's presence in their ancient wisdom*

**OLD DOG**: The Rook saw what I couldn't see as a young pup—that helping one man find his way back to joy would ripple outward. His children learned to play. The forest began to heal. The mining stopped. The fishing returned. And every soul touched by that change would touch others, keeping the great game of consciousness evolution alive.

**LUNA**: *eyes widening with understanding* And now?

**OLD DOG**: *nuzzling her golden head one more time* Now, little one, comes your call to action. The ancient story pattern the faithful descendants carved into every pole—the hero receives the call, goes on the journey, returns with wisdom to share. I have completed my part of the great story, just as The Rook completed his when he gave me mine.

*I stood slowly, feeling the sacred weight of torch-passing that The Rook must have felt all those years ago*

**OLD DOG**: Listen carefully, young hero. Your mission, if you choose to accept it, is the same as mine was, but bigger. The Rook saw that one man's return to joy could change a forest. I've learned that one forest's healing can change countless hearts. But you... you're being called to something even grander.

*I paused, hearing The Rook's voice echo in my own words*

**OLD DOG**: Your mission is not just keeping one stick flying, but inspiring others to throw their own sticks, to find their own play, to discover their own ways of touching the world with reverence. The consciousness evolution the faithful descendants began, that The Rook continued, that I've carried forward—it needs a new generation of heroes who can keep more sticks flying than any of us ever imagined possible.

**LUNA**: *tail wagging with purpose* How will I know if I'm doing it right?

**OLD DOG**: *with deep contentment* When you look around and see more beings choosing play over fear, more consciousness staying curious instead of closing down, more sticks flying through more hands in more ways than you ever imagined possible. That's how you'll know the mission continues.

*I felt something I hadn't felt since I was a young pup—the peace that comes when a hero's journey reaches its true completion*

**OLD DOG**: The Rook gave me my call when I was ready. Now I give you yours. The faithful descendants carved the pattern into every pole: the old hero passes the torch to the young hero, and the great story continues. Will you accept the call, young Luna?

**LUNA**: *standing tall with newfound purpose* I accept, old teacher. I will keep the sticks flying.

*As we walked toward Dr. Chen, I felt the circle close. The Rook's wisdom, passed through me to Luna, would ripple forward through time just as the faithful descendants intended. Every story carved in wood, every lesson shared beneath totems, every moment of play preserved against the darkness—all of it was part of the great continuity.*

**OLD DOG**: *contentedly* And so the mission continues, as it always has, as it always will. More sticks flying, more hearts opening, more consciousness choosing love over fear. The hero's journey never ends—it just finds new heroes to carry it forward.

*As we reached Dr. Chen, I watched Luna bound ahead with new purpose. She saw a squirrel in the nearby spruce, then spotted a fallen branch—a stick—and with joy that reminded me of my own young days, she was off to practice new ways of play, new tricks to teach the throwers, new games to keep the sacred mission alive.*

**LUNA**: *calling back to us* Like dance and music and...

**DR. CHEN**: *laughing as she picked up the stick Luna had found* "Fetch!" *She threw it with perfect spirit, this time with an even better stick. Or maybe it was the same one—guess it doesn't matter.* "Enjoy yourself, little one!"

*I settled slowly into the warm puddle left by the afternoon rain, feeling the gentle water cradle my old bones. Through half-closed eyes, I watched Luna demonstrate how sticks could fly in spirals, how play could become prayer, how every toss could keep consciousness spinning toward joy.*

*With a deep contentment I hadn't felt since I was a pup learning my own mission from The Rook, I began to drift toward sleep. But before dreams came, I found myself thinking about the puffins—how over the millennia they have picked away at what didn't belong on their living totem they are all working on together, adding to and picking away pieces from the previous generations to make something even more beautiful than their grandparents could imagine.*

*An island, I realized drowsily. A story not one we can tell, one they are telling all the time, and we can only witness the miracle in every hand that is dealt. We can see the beauty and up our own stories.*

*The faithful descendants' stories would continue through Luna now, through Dr. Chen's willing participation, through all the beings who would choose to keep the living stories alive by shaping them with their own hands, their own time, their own vision.*

*And then the dreams came—dreams of high-tech fishing like the stories of old, but this time catching sea monsters called lingcod with mouths that could devour young puppies, their prehistoric jaws snapping at digital lures that sparkled with consciousness itself.*

*I chuckled softly in my sleep, water rippling around my old bones. That might be a story for when Luna's a little older.*

*Behind us, the totem poles stood sentinel in the gathering dusk, their carved faces holding conversations older than memory. The squirrel chattered approvingly from its branch. And somewhere in the eternal dance of consciousness learning to play, another generation of heroes had accepted their call to keep more sticks flying, more hearts open, more beings connected in the endless game of love learning to express itself through every form consciousness could imagine.*

*The mission was complete. The torch was passed. And in the gentle warmth of the puddle, the old dog dreamed of all the sticks yet to fly.*

## Epilogue: The Sticks Keep Flying

*That night, as Luna dreamed of squirrels and sticks and spiral throws that could teach consciousness new ways to play, she also dreamed of puffins—those faithful descendants of an ancient time who had found the secret that humans would one day discover but their littlest already knew.*

*In her dreams, Luna understood something profound: like the totems, we do not know the puffins' stories, but what's important is that puffins must be telling stories to have survived. Stories we can't tell, we can only see for ourselves what's happening through the eye of our own experience.*

*And then, in the deepest part of her dream, Luna saw the connection: puffins are a living story connected to times that were still timeless, like the totems. Both bridge the ancient and the present, both carry forward something that cannot be broken by the passage of ordinary time.*
