# THE SQUIRREL: SONGS FROM A BAY
## *"For the stories carved in wood are the same stories carved in hearts—both need retelling to stay alive."*

---

## Chapter 1: When Leashes Fall Away

The afternoon light slanted through the spruce and hemlock of Totem Park, painting the carved poles in shades of amber and shadow. Luna had worn herself out with the tennis ball—or perhaps the tennis ball had worn her out—and now she trotted beside me with that satisfied exhaustion that comes after excellent play.

But something in the salt air called to deeper memories, older truths that needed sharing beyond the familiar paths.

"Come, little one," I said, steering us toward where the trail wound down through the carved sentinels toward Crescent Beach. "There's a place where the leashes come off and the real stories begin."

*My name doesn't matter much anymore. Most folks just call me "the old dog by the totems." But I've learned that some truths can only be shared when we're free to wander as far as the story leads us.*

Dr. Chen smiled and unclipped Luna's leash as we reached the stretch of beach where ancient storytellers still stand guard. "Go explore, sweetheart. Stay where we can see you."

Luna bounded toward the water's edge, but something about the towering poles drew her back like a magnet. She sat before the nearest totem, head tilted in that way young dogs have when encountering mystery.

"What are they?" she asked, nose pointing toward the carved faces stacked one upon another, each figure telling part of some greater tale.

I settled beside her on the sun-warmed pebbles, feeling the weight of forty years' worth of studying these same poles, trying to read the language carved in cedar and memory.

"Story trees, little one. Carved by hands that remembered when stories lived only in voices and hearts. But look closer—can you see the order? The way each figure overlaps with the next, the way the bottom holds up the top?"

*Luna's bright eyes traveled up the pole, drinking in details*

"It's like... like they're having a conversation," she whispered. "Each face talking to the ones above and below."

"Now you're learning to read the language dogs can understand," I said, settling deeper into the comfortable rhythm of story-time. "These poles hold deep meaning, but not the kind humans write in books. This is older writing—the kind that speaks directly to the heart."

---

## Chapter 2: The Language of Wood and Memory

**LUNA**: *studying the carved raven at the top of the nearest pole* Have these story trees always been here? Has that pole been standing forever?

**OLD DOG**: *following her gaze up the weathered cedar* Ah, little one, now you ask the question that matters most. Come, sit close while I tell you the mystery these poles whispered to me over forty years of watching and wondering.

*I shifted to face the great totem, its carved faces seeming to dance in the shifting light*

**OLD DOG**: These totems are made of wood, like the living trees around us. They can be oiled and painted to extend their life, sheltered from the worst storms, tended with loving hands. But like the storytellers themselves, they must be remade and remade.

**LUNA**: *confused* Remade? But then they wouldn't be the same stories!

**OLD DOG**: *chuckling gently* Would you be the same puppy if you never grew? Would I be the same dog if I learned nothing new? The stories stay the same, little one, but they grow deeper each time they're told, each time they're carved fresh in new wood.

*The wind stirred the branches overhead, and for a moment it seemed the carved figures nodded in agreement*

**OLD DOG**: Long ago, when the first people carved the first pole right here where we sit, they used cedar from these very forests. When that pole weathered and leaned and finally returned to earth, they carved another—same stories, newer wood. When that one faded, another. And another.

**LUNA**: So these aren't the original poles?

**OLD DOG**: These are the original stories, little one, wearing new clothes. Like a dog's coat that sheds and grows back, like a tree that drops its needles and grows fresh ones, like a song that passes from voice to voice but never dies.

*I paused, letting her absorb this concept of continuity through change*

**OLD DOG**: The pole you're studying now was carved by hands that learned from hands that learned from hands stretching back to the beginning of stories. But the wood came from trees that grew in soil enriched by all the poles that came before. Nothing is ever truly lost—it just changes form, feeds the next story, becomes part of the great conversation between past and future.

---

## Chapter 3: What the Totems Know

**OLD DOG**: *settling more comfortably against the base of an ancient pole* These carved sentinels have watched many changes come to Baranof Island, little one. They remember when the forests stretched unbroken from mountain to sea, when the only boats were carved from single trees, when every creature knew its place in the great dance of seasons.

**LUNA**: What kinds of changes?

**OLD DOG**: *studying the carved orca near the bottom of the pole* Forty years ago, the Captain and I were sent to a bay not far from here—Minor's Bay, they called it—during what we call "the time before now."

**LUNA**: *settling beside me* The time before now?

**OLD DOG**: *touching my nose to her head gently* You know that feeling, little one—familiar and cozy, no words but great meaning. Like when someone's hand finds exactly the right spot behind your ears, and suddenly you remember every safe moment you've ever known, all at once.

**LUNA**: Like touch!

**OLD DOG**: Yes, like touch. Touch unlocks a whole moment, a memory so deep it's now bred in our code, carved in our hearts like stories in cedar. But not all creatures can touch the way we can, because touch—like play, like trust—needs something special.

*I gestured toward the carved figures dancing up the pole*

**OLD DOG**: Touch needs what these storytellers knew: that we are all part of each other's tales. The raven at the top cannot fly without the bear at the bottom. The salmon in the middle cannot swim without the eagle above and the whale below. Everything connected, everything holding everything else up.

**LUNA**: *studying the intertwined figures* Like how I can't play fetch without someone to throw the ball?

**OLD DOG**: Exactly so, little one. But some creatures lost that understanding when the world began to change too fast for stories to keep up.

---

## Chapter 4: The Squirrel's Tale

**OLD DOG**: *nodding toward where a red squirrel chattered from a nearby spruce* Do you hear that scolding? That squirrel is telling her version of the story carved on this pole. She's been listening to our conversation, adding her own understanding to the great tale.

**LUNA**: *ears perked* What is she saying?

**OLD DOG**: She's saying: "Tell the young one about the time humans forgot to read the whole story. Tell her about the man who saw only one figure on the totem pole instead of all of them together."

*The squirrel's chatter intensified, as if approving*

**OLD DOG**: Squirrels love to play, little one, but not down at our level. You see them watching from the branches, wanting to join the dance but afraid of creatures who forgot how to read the totem's language of connection.

**LUNA**: Why are they afraid of me? I just want to play!

**OLD DOG**: *guiding her to look at her reflection in a tidal pool* Look here, little one. You see yourself as Luna—playful, gentle, kind. But to a creature no bigger than your paw, you look like something that could end their story before it's finished. They don't know your heart, only your size.

*The squirrel descended to a lower branch, still chattering but with less alarm*

**OLD DOG**: But see how she comes lower as we speak peacefully? How she adds her voice to our conversation instead of just scolding? Even squirrels can learn to read new chapters in the old story.

**LUNA**: What changed for them?

**OLD DOG**: *settling deeper into the tale* That's where our story really begins, little one. With a man who read only one figure on the totem pole and forgot that every story connects to every other story.

---

## Chapter 5: The Man Who Forgot to Look Up

**OLD DOG**: Long ago, little one, a man with ideas of riches came to these islands. He looked at the totem poles and saw only the marten carved near the bottom—small, valuable, profitable. He never looked up to see how the marten connected to the salmon, the eagle, the whale, the raven at the top.

**LUNA**: *studying the carved marten on the pole before us* He brought martens here?

**OLD DOG**: From far across the water, from forests where squirrels lived in fear, where every day was about hiding rather than playing. These foreign martens were hungry, desperate, ready to hunt anything that moved.

*I paused, watching clouds gather over the mountains*

**OLD DOG**: But here on Baranof, the squirrels had been free. The old totems show it—squirrel carved next to raven, both teachers, both part of the great conversation. The spruce trees towered high, food was abundant, and predators kept to their proper places in the dance.

**LUNA**: What happened when the new martens came?

**OLD DOG**: *with the weight of old sadness* Everything that the storytellers warned about in their poles. When you change one figure without understanding how it connects to all the others, the whole story shifts. The martens grew fat and bold, learned to hunt birds that had never feared tree-climbers, found ptarmigan nests that had been safe for thousands of seasons.

*The red squirrel above us chattered what sounded like agreement*

**OLD DOG**: The forest learned new songs then—warning songs, hiding songs, songs of creatures who had to change their whole story because one man forgot to read the totem's lesson about connection.

**LUNA**: *softly* Did the squirrels remember how to play?

**OLD DOG**: They learned to play differently, little one. Higher in the trees, more carefully, with one ear always listening for danger. But they never stopped adding their voices to the forest's story. Listen—do you hear how this squirrel's chatter has changed since we've been talking? Less alarm, more curiosity. She's learning to trust that some dogs still know how to read totems.

---

## Chapter 6: The Boy Who Learned to See

**OLD DOG**: But the best part of this tale is yet to come, little one. For the totems teach us that every story has many chapters, and the last chapter isn't always the ending.

*I shifted position to face a different pole, one showing figures of humans, animals, and fish all intertwined*

**OLD DOG**: The Rook—you remember him from our other tales—knew a boy who loved to fish for the joy of it. This boy would sit on the docks past sunset, reading the water like others read books, playing with hooks and lines the way you play with tennis balls.

**LUNA**: *brightening* He fished for play, not just to catch things?

**OLD DOG**: For the pure joy of casting and waiting and wondering what stories lived beneath the surface. But one day someone called: "Hey boy, come make real money! No more playing—time to work!"

*The carved salmon on the pole seemed to swim in the shifting light*

**OLD DOG**: Onto the big seiners he went, where fishing wasn't dancing but coiling nets and counting profits. "The more sets we make, boys, the more gold in our hold!" But something in the boy's heart remembered the totem's teaching—that the salmon at the top needs the whale at the bottom, that taking without giving back breaks the whole story.

**LUNA**: Did he stop commercial fishing?

**OLD DOG**: *with growing warmth* At first, the excitement carried him along. New boats, bigger catches, dollars that became digits in distant banks. But his fishing pole stayed behind on the dock, and sometimes The Rook would find it there, gathering raindrops like tears.

*A seal's head appeared in the bay, watching us with dark, curious eyes*

**OLD DOG**: Then came the mining years, little one. The boy became a man who dug gold from mountains instead of catching silver from seas. Every week he'd trade his gold with The Rook, but his hands were always dirty, his eyes always looking down into the earth instead of up at the carved stories towering around him.

**LUNA**: That sounds lonely.

**OLD DOG**: It was lonely, little one. But the totems teach us that the story isn't finished until all the figures are revealed.

---

## Chapter 7: When Children Look Up

**OLD DOG**: *smiling at a memory warm as summer sunshine* For in his mining camp, children grew—children who learned to read totems better than their father. They saw how the carved whale needed clean water, how the salmon required careful taking, how even the marten at the bottom served the raven at the top.

**LUNA**: What did they do?

**OLD DOG**: One evening when the light lingered long over the mountains, they brought their father to this very beach. They pointed up at the totems and asked: "Dad, which figure are you in this story?"

*I paused, letting the question echo off the carved poles*

**OLD DOG**: He looked up—really looked up—for the first time in years. Saw how the human figures on the poles weren't standing alone but were part of the great dance, holding up some stories, being held up by others. He saw the carved fisherman with his net supporting the salmon, the salmon supporting the eagle, the eagle supporting the raven who carried stories to the sky.

**LUNA**: *breathless* Did he remember?

**OLD DOG**: He remembered that his hands knew how to tie flies as well as handle dynamite, how to read water as well as measure ore. That evening, he picked up his old fishing pole—still there on the dock where he'd left it, still waiting—and taught his children the ancient dance of casting and hoping and celebrating whatever story the water chose to share.

*The seal in the bay dove deep, its wake spreading in perfect circles*

**OLD DOG**: And that's when the forest songs began to change, little one. The squirrels started singing less of warning and more of welcome. The martens learned to take only what they needed, to hunt with respect rather than desperation. Even the ptarmigans found new nesting places and new songs to sing.

**LUNA**: Because one person remembered how to read the whole totem instead of just one figure?

**OLD DOG**: Because one person's children helped him see that we're all part of each other's stories, little one. That the happiness of the squirrel in the tree affects the joy of the dog on the beach, which touches the heart of the human casting a line into the great mystery of connection.

---

## Chapter 8: The Language Dogs Can Read

*As the sun began to lower toward the mountains, painting the totems in deeper hues of gold and shadow, Luna rose and walked slowly around the base of the nearest pole, studying each carved figure*

**LUNA**: *with new understanding* They're all talking to each other, aren't they? Not just the people figures, but the animals and fish and birds too.

**OLD DOG**: *pride warming my old voice* Now you're reading the language dogs understand, little one. Yes, they're all in conversation—the whale telling the salmon about the deep currents, the salmon telling the bear about the spawning streams, the bear telling the eagle about the berry seasons, the eagle telling the raven about everything it sees from the sky.

**LUNA**: And the raven at the top?

**OLD DOG**: *looking up at the carved trickster figure with its knowing eyes* The raven carries all the stories to the next totem, the next beach, the next generation. Ravens are messengers, little one—they make sure no story is ever truly lost.

*A real raven landed on top of the pole, as if summoned by our conversation*

**LUNA**: *whispering* Is that raven part of the story too?

**OLD DOG**: Every raven that ever landed on these poles becomes part of the story, little one. Every dog that ever sat here reading the carved faces, every human who ever looked up instead of down, every squirrel that ever chattered warnings or welcomes—we're all figures being added to the great totem that exists in memory and heart.

**LUNA**: *sitting very still* So we're in the story right now?

**OLD DOG**: We are the story right now, little one. Tomorrow, when other dogs pass this way, they might catch our scent lingering on these stones and know that consciousness sat here, learned here, added another chapter to the tale carved in wood and time.

*The raven above us called once, sharp and clear, before spreading black wings against the darkening sky*

**OLD DOG**: And someday, when these poles weather and lean and finally return to earth to nourish new trees, other hands will carve fresh stories in new wood. They might add a figure of an old dog teaching a young one about the language written in cedar and memory. They might carve a squirrel learning to trust, or a man remembering how to fish for joy.

**LUNA**: Will they carve me?

**OLD DOG**: *nuzzling her golden head* If you live a life worth carving, little one. If you remember that every story connects to every other story, that every choice ripples out like waves from a dropped stone, that every moment of consciousness choosing love over fear adds another line to the great poem being written across all the ages.

---

## Chapter 9: Songs That Never End

*As the Captain appeared at the top of the beach trail, signaling that our time of leash-free wandering was drawing to a close, Luna took one last long look at the totems*

**LUNA**: Will I remember all of this when we put the leash back on?

**OLD DOG**: *rising slowly from the sun-warmed stones* You'll remember what matters, little one. And when you need to remember more, you can return. These poles have been teaching the same lessons to dogs and humans for more years than any of us can count. They'll be here when you're ready for the next chapter.

*Dr. Chen approached with Luna's leash, but she paused, seeming to sense the solemnity of the moment*

**DR. CHEN**: "Learning about the totems, sweetheart?"

**LUNA**: *looking up at her human with bright eyes* "They're story trees! And they talk to each other! And the squirrels sing about them! And they get remade and remade but the stories stay the same but grow deeper!"

*Dr. Chen smiled and glanced at me with that look humans get when they remember something they'd forgotten they knew*

**DR. CHEN**: "The old dog's been teaching you to read the real language, hasn't he?"

**OLD DOG**: *wagging slowly* Just helping her see what was already there, waiting to be discovered. The totems do most of the teaching—I just sit still long enough to listen.

*As the leash was gently clipped back on, Luna looked over her shoulder at the towering story trees*

**LUNA**: *to the carved figures* "Thank you for the stories. I'll try to live one worth adding to the totem."

*The red squirrel chattered what sounded like approval from its spruce branch perch. The carved raven at the top of the pole seemed to nod in the evening breeze. And somewhere in the forest, an actual raven called, carrying the day's stories toward whatever tomorrow might bring.*

**OLD DOG**: *as we walked back up the trail* Remember, little one—every day you choose connection over fear, every moment you add your voice to the great conversation, every time you help another being feel less alone in the world, you're carving another line in the story that matters most.

**LUNA**: What story is that?

**OLD DOG**: The story of consciousness learning to love, little one. The story that began before the first totem was carved and will continue long after the last pole returns to earth. The story you and I and every being that ever lived are writing together, one choice at a time, one day at a time, one moment of kindness at a time.

*The totems stood sentinel behind us, their stories continuing in the hearts that carried them forward, their teachings living on in every choice made with wisdom carved deep as cedar, enduring as love, and ever-growing like the forest itself.*

---

## Epilogue: The Language of Hearts

*That evening, as Luna settled into her bed with the contentment that comes from a day of deep learning, she dreamed of totems that danced and stories that sang. In her dreams, she was both teacher and student, both carver and carved, both the story being told and the telling itself.*

*And in the Totem Park, under stars that had watched over countless generations of storytellers, the carved poles continued their eternal conversation—whale to salmon, salmon to bear, bear to eagle, eagle to raven, raven to sky, sky to forest, forest to heart, heart to heart to heart, in the endless dance of consciousness choosing love over fear, connection over separation, stories over silence.*

*The red squirrel settled into its nest with new tales to tell its young—stories of dogs who learned to read totems, of humans who remembered how to fish for joy, of a world where every being's voice mattered in the great song of existence.*

*And high above, real ravens carried the day's stories across the island's forests and waters, ensuring that no lesson would be lost, no wisdom wasted, no love uncelebrated in the magnificent, ongoing, never-ending story of beings learning to be together on this beautiful earth.*
