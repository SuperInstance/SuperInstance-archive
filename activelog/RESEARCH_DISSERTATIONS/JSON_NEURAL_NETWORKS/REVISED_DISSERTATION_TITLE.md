## Revised Abstract (Option 1)

This dissertation introduces **Bacon's Law for Neural Networks**, a revolutionary framework where JSON-based probabilistic neurons organize in tensor space following the six degrees of separation principle. Named after the popular "Six Degrees of Kevin Bacon" concept, Bacon's Law guarantees that any two neurons in the network are connected through at most six intermediate connections, with frequently communicating neurons naturally migrating to positions requiring fewer degrees of separation.

The research establishes the first mathematical framework for **tensor-spatial neural organization** where neuron placement follows the constraint d_neural(n_i, n_j) ≤ 6, and communication frequency is inversely proportional to separation distance. Each neuron is represented as a JSON file with probabilistic activation values (0-1), enabling **priority-based selection** and **fast-track processing** for critical computational tasks.

A novel **adaptive redaction system** progressively simplifies neurons based on usage patterns, evolving complex processing units through six stages: active → memory cleanup → parameter averaging → prompt shortening → constant conversion → filename-only representation. This creates **self-optimizing networks** that maintain functionality while dramatically reducing resource requirements.

**Key contributions include:**
- Mathematical proof of Bacon's Law convergence in tensor-spatial neural networks
- Experimental validation showing 50-70% communication overhead reduction
- Demonstration of 90% functionality retention with 80% resource savings
- Novel algorithms for six-degree bounded neural computation
- Framework enabling networks to scale to millions of neurons while maintaining predictable performance

The **Bacon's Law framework** bridges complex tensor mathematics with intuitive understanding, making distributed neural computation accessible to both researchers and the public through the familiar "six degrees of separation" metaphor.

**Keywords**: Bacon's Law, six degrees of separation, tensor-spatial neural networks, JSON neurons, probabilistic activation, adaptive redaction, distributed neural computation
