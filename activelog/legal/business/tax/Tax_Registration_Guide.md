# Tax Registration Guide

## Federal Tax Registration

### 1. Employer Identification Number (EIN)

**Required For:**
- LLCs with multiple members
- Corporations
- Businesses with employees
- Single-member LLCs (optional but recommended)

**How to Apply:**
- **Online (Recommended):** IRS.gov - fastest method
- **Phone:** 1-800-829-4933 (Monday-Friday, 7am-7pm local time)
- **Fax:** Complete Form SS-4 and fax to your state's designated number
- **Mail:** Send completed Form SS-4 to your state's IRS office

**Information Needed:**
- Legal business name
- Trade name (DBA) if different
- Business address
- Mailing address if different
- Responsible party (name, SSN, title)
- Business structure (LLC, Corporation, etc.)
- Reason for applying
- Start date of business
- Expected number of employees

**Processing Time:**
- Online: Immediate
- Phone: During the call
- Fax: 4 business days
- Mail: 4-5 weeks

### 2. Business Activity Codes

**NAICS Code (North American Industry Classification System):**
- 6-digit code describing your primary business activity
- Used for statistical purposes
- Required on tax returns and government forms

**Common NAICS Codes:**
- 541511: Custom Computer Programming Services
- 541512: Computer Systems Design Services
- 541513: Computer Facilities Management Services
- 541519: Other Computer Related Services
- 541611: Administrative Management and General Management Consulting Services

**SIC Code (Standard Industrial Classification):**
- 4-digit code (older system, still used by some agencies)
- Similar to NAICS but less detailed

### 3. Business Tax Elections

**S Corporation Election (Form 2553):**
- Deadline: Within 2 months and 15 days of incorporation
- Pass-through taxation (no corporate tax)
- Owners pay personal income tax on profits
- Potential payroll tax savings

**LLC Tax Elections:**
- **Single-member LLC:** Default = disregarded entity (Schedule C)
- **Multi-member LLC:** Default = partnership (Form 1065)
- **Election to be taxed as Corporation:** Form 8832
- **Election to be taxed as S-Corp:** Form 2553

## State Tax Registration

### Sales Tax Registration

**States Requiring Registration:**
All states except: Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire, Oregon

**When Registration is Required:**
- Selling tangible goods
- Providing taxable services
- Exceeding economic nexus thresholds
- Having physical presence in state

**Economic Nexus Thresholds (2024):**
- **$100,000 in sales OR 200+ transactions:** Most states
- **$500,000 in sales:** California, New York, Texas
- **Varies by state:** Check each state's requirements

**Registration Process:**
1. Determine nexus obligations
2. Register online through state website
3. Obtain sales tax permit/license
4. Set up collection and remittance procedures

### State Income Tax

**States Without Corporate Income Tax:**
- Nevada, South Dakota, Wyoming, Washington

**States Without Individual Income Tax:**
- Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming

**Franchise Tax States:**
- Delaware: $175 minimum
- California: $800 minimum
- Texas: Margin tax based on revenue

**Examples:**
- Chicago: 1.25% city sales tax

## Quarterly Tax Obligations

### Federal Quarterly Taxes (Form 941)

**Due Dates:**
- Q1: April 30
- Q2: July 31  
- Q3: October 31
- Q4: January 31

**Required If:**
- You have employees
- You pay wages subject to federal income tax withholding
- You pay wages subject to FICA tax

### Estimated Tax Payments (Form 1040ES)

**Due Dates:**
- Q1: April 15
- Q2: June 15
- Q3: September 15
- Q4: January 15

**Required If:**
- Self-employment income exceeds $400
- Expected tax liability exceeds $1,000
- Previous year's tax liability exceeded $1,000

**Safe Harbor Rule:**
Pay 100% of last year's tax liability (110% if AGI > $150,000)

### State Quarterly Requirements

**Unemployment Insurance (SUTA):**
- Register when you hire your first employee
- Rates vary by state and industry
- Quarterly reporting and payments required

**Workers' Compensation:**
- Required in most states when you have employees
- Rates based on job classification and payroll
- Some states allow self-insurance

## Annual Tax Filing Requirements

### Federal Annual Returns

**Form 1120 (C Corporation):**
- Due: 15th day of 4th month after year-end
- Extension: 6 months (Form 7004)

**Form 1120S (S Corporation):**
- Due: 15th day of 3rd month after year-end
- Extension: 6 months (Form 7004)

**Form 1065 (Partnership/Multi-member LLC):**
- Due: 15th day of 3rd month after year-end
- Extension: 6 months (Form 7004)

**Schedule C (Single-member LLC/Sole Proprietorship):**
- Filed with personal tax return (Form 1040)
- Due: April 15 (or October 15 with extension)

### State Annual Returns

**Annual Report/Statement:**
- Required in most states
- Due dates vary (often anniversary of incorporation)
- Fees typically $25-$300

**State Tax Returns:**
- Follow federal entity classification or make separate election
- Due dates generally align with federal requirements

## Common Tax Registration Mistakes

### 1. Delayed EIN Application
**Problem:** Waiting until you need to file taxes
**Solution:** Apply immediately after business formation

### 2. Incorrect Business Classification
**Problem:** Choosing wrong entity type for tax purposes
**Solution:** Consult with tax professional before filing

### 3. Missing Nexus Obligations
**Problem:** Not registering for sales tax in required states
**Solution:** Regularly review economic nexus thresholds

### 4. Late S-Corp Election
**Problem:** Missing the 2 month + 15 days deadline
**Solution:** File immediately after incorporation or at start of tax year

### 5. Inadequate Record Keeping
**Problem:** Missing receipts and documentation
**Solution:** Implement digital receipt capture from day one

## Professional Tax Help

### When to Hire a Tax Professional

**Complex Situations:**
- Multi-state operations
- International transactions
- Inventory-based business
- Multiple business entities
- High-volume transactions

**Red Flags:**
- Audit notices
- Large tax liabilities
- Payroll tax issues
- Sales tax compliance problems
- Estimated payment penalties

### Types of Tax Professionals

**CPA (Certified Public Accountant):**
- Can represent you before IRS
- Highest level of training
- Can sign tax returns
- Attorney-client privilege in some states

**Enrolled Agent (EA):**
- IRS representation rights
- Specializes in tax matters
- Less expensive than CPA
- Must pass IRS examination

**Tax Attorney:**
- Legal advice on tax matters
- Complex business structures
- Attorney-client privilege
- Tax litigation

**Tax Preparer:**
- Basic return preparation
- No representation rights
- May require PTIN registration
- Varies widely in expertise

## State-Specific Considerations

### Delaware Corporations

**Franchise Tax:**
- Minimum $175 annually
- Based on authorized shares method or assumed par value method
- Due March 1st
- Penalties for late filing

### California LLC Tax

**Annual Fee:**
- Minimum $800
- Additional fee based on gross receipts
- Due by 15th day of 4th month after year-end
- Applies even if no income

### New York City Business Taxes

**General Corporation Tax:**
- Based on entire net income
- Alternative minimum tax
- Metropolitan Commuter Transportation Mobility Tax

### Texas Franchise Tax

**No Value Test:**
- Businesses with revenue under $1.23M may qualify
- Margin tax for others
- E-Z computation for small businesses

## International Considerations

### Foreign-Owned US Businesses

**Additional Requirements:**
- Form 5472 (25% foreign ownership)
- FIRPTA withholding on real estate
- Transfer pricing documentation
- FATCA reporting

### US Businesses with Foreign Operations

**Reporting Requirements:**
- Form 5471 (foreign corporation ownership)
- Form 8865 (foreign partnership interests)
- FBAR (foreign bank accounts)
- Form 8938 (FATCA)
