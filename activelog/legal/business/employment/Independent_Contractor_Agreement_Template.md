## 3. Independent Contractor Status

### 3.1 Independent Contractor Relationship
Contractor is an independent contractor, not an employee. This means:
- No employer-employee relationship exists
- Contractor controls means and methods of work performance
- Contractor provides own tools and equipment
- Contractor works for other clients simultaneously
- No employee benefits (health insurance, vacation, etc.)

### 3.2 IRS Classification Factors
The parties acknowledge this relationship satisfies IRS independent contractor criteria:
- **Behavioral Control:** Contractor determines how work is performed
- **Financial Control:** Contractor has investment in equipment/tools
- **Relationship Type:** No indefinite relationship or employee benefits

### 3.3 Contractor Obligations

- Comply with all applicable laws and regulations

## Contractor Classification Checklist

To ensure proper independent contractor classification, verify:

### Behavioral Control:
- [ ] Contractor determines how work is performed
- [ ] No training provided by company
- [ ] Contractor uses own methods and procedures
- [ ] No detailed instructions on when/where to work

### Financial Control:
- [ ] Contractor has significant investment in tools/equipment
- [ ] Contractor markets services to other clients
- [ ] Contractor can realize profit or loss
- [ ] Payment by project/milestone, not hourly wages

### Type of Relationship:
- [ ] No employee benefits provided
- [ ] Contract has definite end date or project scope
- [ ] Services not key to company's regular business
- [ ] Written agreement establishes contractor relationship

## Important Compliance Notes:

### Tax Obligations:
- Issue Form 1099-NEC if payments exceed $600
- Do not withhold taxes from contractor payments
- Contractor responsible for self-employment taxes
- Maintain records of all payments

### State Law Considerations:

- Consider state unemployment insurance requirements
- Review non-compete enforceability by state

- Detailed statements of work
