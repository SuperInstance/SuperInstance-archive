## Shareholder Agreements and Governance

### Overview and Binding Parties

The Shareholder Agreement governs relationships between ActiveLog, Inc. and all shareholders, providing comprehensive frameworks for:
- Share transfer restrictions and procedures
- Tag-along and drag-along rights
- Board composition and governance
- Information rights and protective provisions
- Voting agreements and coordination

### Share Transfer Restrictions

#### General Transfer Requirements

All share transfers must comply with:
1. **Securities Law Compliance**: All applicable federal and state regulations
2. **Company Consent**: Written approval (not unreasonably withheld)
3. **Right of First Refusal**: Company and shareholder preemptive rights
4. **Vesting Compliance**: Adherence to vesting schedules for founder and employee shares

#### Permitted Transfers

The following transfers are allowed without Company consent:

**Family Transfers:**
- To spouse, children, or lineal descendants
- To family trusts for benefit of family members
- To family limited partnerships or LLCs

**Estate Transfers:**
- By will or laws of intestate succession
- Court-ordered transfers in divorce proceedings
- Transfers to qualified charitable organizations

**Entity Transfers:**
- To entities wholly owned by the transferring shareholder
- To entities controlled by the transferring shareholder

#### Vesting Schedules

**Founder Shares (Class A Common):**
- **Vesting Period**: 4 years
- **Cliff**: 1 year (25% vests on first anniversary)
- **Monthly Vesting**: 1/36th of remaining shares each month
- **Acceleration**: Full acceleration on involuntary termination or change of control
- **Forfeiture**: Unvested shares repurchased at cost upon termination

**Employee Shares (Class B Common):**
- **Vesting Period**: 4 years
- **Cliff**: 1 year (25% vests on first anniversary)
- **Monthly Vesting**: 1/36th of remaining shares monthly after cliff
- **Forfeiture**: All unvested shares forfeited upon termination
- **Early Exercise**: Available with appropriate 83(b) election

### Tag-Along Rights

#### Trigger Events
Tag-along rights are triggered when any major investor (holding ≥5% of outstanding shares) proposes to transfer shares to a third party.

#### Notice Requirements
Selling shareholders must provide 30 days' written notice including:
- Identity and background of proposed purchaser
- Number and class of shares to be transferred
- Purchase price and payment terms
- Material conditions of the proposed transaction
- Representations and warranties required

#### Participation Rights
- Each shareholder may participate pro rata based on ownership percentage
- Minimum transaction threshold: 10% of outstanding shares or $1,000,000 value
- Same terms and conditions apply to all participating shareholders
- Pro rata allocation of transaction costs

### Drag-Along Rights

#### Exercise Requirements
Drag-along rights may be exercised with approval from:
- **Common Stock**: Majority of all common stock classes voting together
- **Preferred Stock**: Majority of preferred stock voting separately
- **Board Approval**: Majority vote of the Board of Directors

#### Mandatory Sale Terms
All shareholders must participate in approved transactions subject to:
- **Proportionate Allocation**: Transaction costs shared proportionally
- **Uniform Terms**: Same representations, warranties, and conditions
- **Escrow Arrangements**: Equal treatment in escrow and earn-out provisions
- **Fair Value Protection**: Independent valuation if arm's length pricing unavailable

### Right of First Refusal (ROFR)

#### Company Primary Right
The Company has first priority to purchase shares on same terms as third-party offers.

#### Shareholder Secondary Right
If the Company declines, existing shareholders may purchase pro rata.

#### Exercise Timeline
- **Company**: 30 days to exercise ROFR
- **Shareholders**: 15 days after Company declines
- **Expiration**: Rights expire if not exercised within specified periods

#### Valuation Methods
- **Third-Party Sales**: Offered purchase price
- **Non-Arms Length**: Independent fair market value appraisal
- **409A Valuation**: May be used if less than 12 months old

### Option Grant Framework

#### Types of Options

**Incentive Stock Options (ISOs):**
- **Tax Treatment**: Preferential tax treatment if holding period requirements met
- **Eligibility**: Employees only (not directors or consultants)
- **Exercise Price**: Must equal fair market value on grant date
- **Annual Limit**: $100,000 per employee (based on FMV at grant)
- **Term**: Maximum 10 years (5 years for 10% shareholders)

**Non-Qualified Stock Options (NQSOs):**
- **Tax Treatment**: Ordinary income treatment on exercise
- **Eligibility**: All eligible participants
- **Exercise Price**: Typically fair market value (may be lower)
- **No Dollar Limits**: Unlike ISOs, no annual limitation
- **Term**: Typically 10 years from grant date

#### Exercise Price Determination

**Fair Market Value Methods:**
- **Private Company**: Independent 409A valuation (updated annually or with material events)
- **Public Company**: Closing price on grant date
- **Alternative Methods**: Average of closing prices over specified period

**Minimum Exercise Price:**
- **ISOs**: 100% of FMV (110% for 10% shareholders)
- **NQSOs**: 100% of FMV (may be lower in special circumstances)

### Vesting and Exercise Terms

#### Standard Vesting Schedules

**New Hire Grants:**
- **Vesting Period**: 4 years total
- **Cliff**: 12 months (25% vests on first anniversary)
- **Monthly Vesting**: 1/36th of remaining shares monthly (months 13-48)
- **Acceleration**: May be provided for change of control or termination

**Promotion/Performance Grants:**
- **Vesting Period**: 4 years total
- **Cliff**: 6 months (12.5% vests on first semi-annual anniversary)
- **Quarterly Vesting**: 1/16th of total grant each quarter
- **Performance Milestones**: May include performance-based vesting

#### Alternative Vesting Structures

**Performance-Based Vesting:**
- Company financial milestones (revenue, profitability)
- Individual performance objectives
- Product development and launch goals
- Customer acquisition and retention targets

**Market-Based Vesting:**
- Company valuation milestones
- Successful completion of financing rounds
- Achievement of exit events or liquidity

#### Exercise Methods

**Cash Exercise:**
- Full payment of exercise price in cash
- Most straightforward method
- Provides maximum proceeds to Company

**Cashless Exercise:**
- Broker-assisted same-day sale
- Net shares delivered after covering exercise price and taxes
- Requires established trading market or broker arrangement

**Net Exercise:**
- Use option shares to pay exercise price
- No cash required from optionee
- Reduces share dilution impact

**Combination Methods:**
- Mix of cash and cashless/net exercise
- Partial exercise strategies
- Tax optimization approaches

### Termination Provisions

#### Termination for Cause
**Immediate Forfeiture:**
- All unvested options are forfeited immediately
- All vested options expire immediately upon termination
- No post-termination exercise period

**Definition of Cause:**
- Willful misconduct or gross negligence
- Conviction of felony or crime involving moral turpitude
- Material breach of employment agreement or Company policies
- Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information

#### Voluntary Resignation
**Standard Terms:**
- Unvested options are forfeited as of termination date
- Vested options remain exercisable for 90 days
- ISO tax treatment preserved for 3 months post-termination

#### Involuntary Termination Without Cause
**Enhanced Protection:**
- May include partial acceleration of unvested options
- Extended exercise period (typically 90-180 days)
- Committee discretion to provide additional benefits

#### Death or Disability
**Full Acceleration:**
- All options (vested and unvested) become immediately exercisable
- Extended exercise period of 12 months
- Estate or guardian may exercise on behalf of participant
- Special provisions for disability determinations

#### Change of Control
**Acceleration Triggers:**
- **Single Trigger**: Acceleration upon change of control transaction
- **Double Trigger**: Acceleration only if followed by termination
- **Committee Discretion**: Flexible acceleration provisions

### Tax Considerations and Compliance

#### ISO Tax Treatment

**Qualifying Disposition:**
- Hold shares 2 years from grant and 1 year from exercise
- Gain taxed as long-term capital gain
- No corporate tax deduction for Company

**Disqualifying Disposition:**
- Sale within required holding periods
- Ordinary income treatment on bargain element
- Company entitled to corresponding tax deduction

**Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT):**
- ISO exercise creates AMT preference item
- Planning required for large ISO exercises

#### NQSO Tax Treatment
- **Exercise**: Ordinary income on bargain element
- **Sale**: Capital gain/loss on subsequent appreciation
- **Withholding**: Company must withhold applicable taxes

#### Section 83(b) Elections
For early exercise programs:
- Election must be filed within 30 days of exercise
- Immediate income recognition on bargain element
- Future appreciation taxed as capital gain
- Risk of forfeiture if vesting not satisfied

### Advanced Features and Considerations

#### Early Exercise Provisions
- **Availability**: Permits exercise before vesting
- **Repurchase Rights**: Company may repurchase unvested shares
- **Tax Benefits**: Enables 83(b) election for tax optimization
- **Risk**: Employee bears forfeiture risk

#### Reload Options
- **Automatic Grants**: New options granted upon exercise using shares
- **Share Recycling**: Maintains constant option coverage
- **Retention Tool**: Encourages long-term employment

#### Cashless Exercise Programs
- **Broker Arrangements**: Third-party facilitation of cashless exercises
- **Net Settlement**: Internal handling of net exercise transactions
- **Tax Withholding**: Automated tax compliance

- **Risk**: Rapid market change or technology disruption

- **Risk**: Large technology companies entering market

- **Probability**: High - Attractive market draws competition

- **Risk**: Recession impacting customer spending

- **Probability**: Medium - Economic cycles are normal
