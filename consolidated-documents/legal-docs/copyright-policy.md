## 2. DMCA Compliance Framework

### 2.1 Safe Harbor Provisions

ActiveLog qualifies for protection under the DMCA's "safe harbor" provisions as a service provider that:
- Does not have actual knowledge of infringing activity
- Does not receive financial benefit directly attributable to infringing activity
- Responds expeditiously to copyright infringement notices
- Has adopted and reasonably implemented a repeat infringer policy
- Accommodates and does not interfere with standard technical measures

### 2.2 Our Commitment

We are committed to:
- Promptly processing valid DMCA takedown notices

## 3. Copyright Infringement Notification Process

### 3.1 Filing a DMCA Takedown Notice

If you believe that content on ActiveLog infringes your copyright, you may submit a DMCA takedown notice. To be effective under the DMCA, your notice must include:

#### Required Information:

1. **Physical or Electronic Signature**
   - Physical signature of the copyright owner or authorized agent
   - Electronic signature (typed name) is acceptable for electronic submissions

2. **Identification of Copyrighted Work**
   - Clear identification of the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed
   - For multiple works: a representative list of such works

3. **Identification of Infringing Material**
   - Specific identification of material claimed to be infringing
   - Information sufficient to permit us to locate the material (URLs, repository names, specific file paths)

4. **Contact Information**
   - Your name, address, telephone number, and email address
   - Information sufficient for us to contact you regarding the complaint

5. **Good Faith Statement**
   - Statement that you have a good faith belief that the disputed use is not authorized by:
     - The copyright owner
     - The copyright owner's agent
     - The law (including fair use)

6. **Accuracy Statement**
   - Statement that the information in the notification is accurate
   - Statement under penalty of perjury that you are:
     - The copyright owner, or
     - Authorized to act on behalf of the copyright owner

#### Example DMCA Notice Template:

```
To: dmca@activelog.com
Subject: DMCA Takedown Notice

I, [Your Name], am the [owner/authorized agent] of certain intellectual property rights ("IP Owner").

1. COPYRIGHTED WORK:
   I own rights to the following copyrighted work(s):
   - [Description of copyrighted work]
   - [Registration number, if applicable]
   - [Publication date, if applicable]

2. INFRINGING MATERIAL:
   The following material on your service infringes my copyright:
   - URL: [Specific URL of infringing content]
   - Repository: [Repository name and path]
   - Description: [Brief description of how it infringes]

3. CONTACT INFORMATION:
   Name: [Your full name]
   Address: [Your address]
   Phone: [Your phone number]
   Email: [Your email address]

4. GOOD FAITH STATEMENT:
   I have a good faith belief that the disputed use described above is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.

5. ACCURACY STATEMENT:
   I swear, under penalty of perjury, that the information in this notification is accurate and that I am the copyright owner or am authorized to act on behalf of the owner of an exclusive right that is allegedly infringed.

Signature: [Your signature]
Date: [Date]
```

## 4. Counter-Notification Process

### 4.1 User Rights

If you believe your content was removed in error or misidentification, you may file a DMCA counter-notification. This is your legal right under the DMCA to challenge a takedown.

### 4.2 Counter-Notice Requirements

Your counter-notification must include:

1. **Physical or Electronic Signature**
2. **Identification of Removed Material**
   - Description of material that was removed
   - Location where material appeared before removal
3. **Good Faith Statement**
   - Statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good faith belief that the material was removed due to mistake or misidentification
4. **Consent to Jurisdiction**
   - Statement that you consent to jurisdiction of Federal District Court for your address (or Northern District of California if outside US)
   - Statement that you will accept service of process from the complainant
5. **Contact Information**
   - Your name, address, and telephone number

#### Counter-Notice Template:

```
To: dmca@activelog.com
Subject: DMCA Counter-Notification

1. IDENTIFICATION:
   My name is: [Your name]
   My address is: [Your address]
   My telephone number is: [Your phone]
   My email address is: [Your email]

2. REMOVED MATERIAL:
   The material that was removed was located at:
   [Specific URLs or locations of removed content]
   
   Description of removed material:
   [Brief description of your content]

3. GOOD FAITH STATEMENT:
   I swear, under penalty of perjury, that I have a good faith belief that the material identified above was removed as a result of a mistake or misidentification of the material to be removed or disabled.

4. JURISDICTION:
   I consent to the jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for the judicial district in which my address is located, or if my address is outside of the United States, the judicial district in which ActiveLog is located, and I will accept service of process from the claimant.

Signature: [Your signature]
Date: [Date]
```

### 4.3 Counter-Notice Process

1. **Submission:** Send counter-notice to dmca@activelog.com
2. **Review:** We review for completeness and validity
3. **Forward to Complainant:** Valid counter-notices are forwarded to original complainant
4. **Restoration Timeline:** Content may be restored in 10-14 business days unless complainant files court action
5. **Court Action:** If complainant files lawsuit, content remains down pending court resolution

## 5. Repeat Infringer Policy

### 5.1 Policy Overview

ActiveLog maintains a policy of terminating user accounts that are determined to be repeat infringers of copyrighted material. This policy helps us maintain safe harbor protection under the DMCA.

### 5.2 Infringer Determination

We consider users to be repeat infringers when:
- Multiple valid DMCA takedown notices are received regarding their content
- Pattern of uploading copyrighted material without authorization is established
- Previous warnings and educational efforts have been ineffective
- User has failed to respond appropriately to takedown notices

### 5.3 Progressive Enforcement

**First Offense:**
- Content removal
- Educational email about copyright law
- Warning about repeat infringer policy
- Account notation

**Second Offense:**
- Content removal
- Formal warning letter
- Temporary account restrictions
- Required copyright education module

**Third Offense:**
- Content removal
- Final warning
- Extended account restrictions
- Mandatory review process

**Subsequent Offenses:**
- Account termination
- Permanent ban from service
- Forfeiture of paid subscriptions

### 5.4 Appeals Process

Users may appeal repeat infringer determinations by:
- Providing evidence of authorization to use copyrighted material
- Demonstrating that takedown notices were invalid or abusive
- Showing that content falls under fair use or other exceptions
- Completing copyright education and compliance training

## 6. Fair Use and Legal Use Considerations

### 6.1 Fair Use Doctrine

We recognize that not all use of copyrighted material constitutes infringement. Fair use factors include:
- Purpose and character of use (commercial vs. educational)
- Nature of the copyrighted work
- Amount and substantiality of portion used
- Effect on the market for the original work

### 6.2 Educational Use

ActiveLog supports legitimate educational use of copyrighted material:
- Code examples for learning purposes
- Commentary and criticism
- Transformative use in educational projects
- Properly attributed academic work

### 6.3 Open Source and Public Domain

We encourage use of:
- Open source software with proper licensing
- Public domain materials
- Creative Commons licensed content
- Properly licensed third-party libraries

## 7. Special Considerations for Code Repositories

### 7.1 Software Copyright

Software code is protected by copyright, including:
- Source code and object code
- Comments and documentation
- Configuration files
- Build scripts and deployment tools
- API definitions and schemas

### 7.2 Common Issues

**Forked Repositories:**
- Ensure original repository has appropriate license
- Respect license terms and attribution requirements
- Understand difference between forking and copying

**Third-Party Libraries:**
- Check licenses of all dependencies
- Include proper license files and attributions
- Understand GPL, MIT, Apache, and other common licenses

**Enterprise Code:**
- Do not upload proprietary employer code
- Respect employment agreements and NDAs
- Obtain proper authorization for work-related projects

### 7.3 Best Practices

- Always check licenses before using third-party code
- Include LICENSE files in your repositories
- Provide proper attribution for borrowed code
- When in doubt, create original implementations
- Use dependency scanners to check for license conflicts

## 8. False Claims and Abuse Prevention

### 8.1 Anti-Abuse Measures

We take steps to prevent abuse of the DMCA process:
- Review takedown notices for validity and completeness
- Identify patterns of questionable claims
- Investigate potential false or misleading notices
- Take action against users who file fraudulent claims

### 8.2 Penalties for False Claims

Under DMCA Section 512(f), knowingly making false claims may result in:
- Liability for damages including attorney fees
- Perjury charges for false statements under oath
- Termination of ActiveLog account privileges
- Legal action for abuse of process

### 8.3 Bad Faith Indicators

We monitor for signs of bad faith, including:
- Claims without legitimate copyright ownership
- Automated or bulk filings without proper review
- Claims targeting legitimate fair use or criticism
- Attempts to circumvent terms of service
- Claims filed to gain competitive advantage
